# User Success Definition

**Phase:** 2 — UX Documentation (added per Phase 1 review feedback)
**Status:** Draft for review
**Purpose:** Define success **from the user's perspective, independent of
business metrics**. `../product/SuccessMetrics.md` measures the product;
this document defines what winning feels like in a user's life — the
standard against which even good metrics must be sanity-checked. If the
metrics say yes and this document says no, this document wins.

---

## 1. The One-Sentence Definition

> **The user notices their days, starts what matters to them with less
> internal struggle, and trusts themselves a little more — whether or not
> they still use Wake.**

Three components, deliberately ordered: perception (time is felt), action
(starting got smaller), and self-relationship (the guilt spiral lost its
compounding term). The final clause is the countercultural one: user
success is explicitly independent of continued app usage.

## 2. Success From Inside (first-person markers)

What users would actually say, stage by stage (`EmotionalJourney.md`):

- *Early:* "It asked me one question and then just… worked."
  "Two minutes felt doable, and weirdly, it was."
- *Middle:* "I can feel where the afternoon is now." "When I hear the
  time, I sort of surface." "I disappeared for a week and it didn't
  punish me. I came back."
- *Mature:* "Starting isn't really a *thing* for me anymore." "I still
  glance at the dots. I don't need to, but I like them." "I'd tell a
  friend: it's the only one of these apps that never once made me feel
  bad."

That last sentence is the product's reputation target, verbatim.

## 3. The Success Ladder (user-perspective milestones)

| Rung | The user's experience | NOT the milestone |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A felt day | Today has a shape; its passage registers without dread | Checking a dashboard |
| 2. A believed ask | "Two minutes" reads as true, because it always was | Complying with pressure |
| 3. A banked start | One real start whose relief was noticed | A logged session |
| 4. A survived lapse | Absence, then return, with zero owed | An unbroken streak |
| 5. A shrunken wall | The gap between "I should" and "I am" narrows in daily life, beyond the app's presence | More app opens |
| 6. A revised story | "I'm someone who starts" replaces "I'm a procrastinator" | A badge that says so |
| 7. Autonomy | Time-feel and small starts persist unaided; Wake is optional | Retention |

Each rung's right-hand column is the industry's usual proxy — listed to
keep us honest about the difference.

## 4. What Success Is NOT (user-perspective anti-definitions)

- **Not app engagement.** A user opening Wake twelve times a day is a
  warning sign (checking as new avoidance — `AntiGoals.md` A2), not a
  power user.
- **Not maximal starts.** Success is starting *what matters when it
  matters*, including choosing rest legitimately (T4). A user gaming
  starts-per-day for its own sake has replaced one scorekeeping anxiety
  with another (A4).
- **Not becoming "productive" as identity.** Wake succeeds when work
  fits into a life, not when life reorganizes around output (A5). The
  word "productivity" stays out of our vocabulary for this reason.
- **Not needing Wake forever.** Dependence that deepens over time is the
  model failing (BehavioralDesign §7).

## 5. Per-Persona Success Snapshots

- **Maya:** the thesis moves most weekdays — not heroically, in 2-to-20
  minute bites that often grow; the 23:00 guilt session has largely
  disappeared because there is nothing to audit. She survived two dead
  weeks without abandoning the tool or herself.
- **Daniel:** the side project sees commits again; "not enough time
  tonight" lost to the evening dots showing exactly enough time for one
  real thing. He stopped at 120 seconds many times — and learned that
  was true, so the button stayed believable.
- **Priya:** invoices go out the week they're earned; the spoken time
  surfaces her from hyperfocus in time to eat; nothing in the app has
  ever once made her flinch. Her word for it: "safe."
- **Tomás:** the deck exists; the calls got made in 2-minute ignitions
  that became 40-minute sessions. He watches less motivational content —
  the itch it scratched is being scratched by actual starts.

## 6. How We'll Know (without violating our principles)

User-perspective success is verified through consented research, not
surveillance (P11): the H8 sentiment pulse, Wave 4 beta interviews,
lapsed-user interviews (a lapsed user reporting rung 5–7 outcomes is a
*success story*, recorded as such), and review/support language. The
graduation guardrail (self-initiated share rising —
`../product/SuccessMetrics.md` §5) is the only telemetry proxy for rung 7,
and it is read as good news.

## 7. The Standing Sanity Check

At every phase gate and metrics review, one question is asked against
this document: **"Are users winning by their own definition, or only by
ours?"** Any initiative that improves product metrics while moving users
down this ladder (more engagement, more dependence, more scorekeeping) is
rejected under the constitution's conflict rule.

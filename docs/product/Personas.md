## Persona 1 — Maya, 24 — Graduate student (Segment S1)

**Situation.** Master's thesis due in four months. No classes, no boss —
just a document that has been "in progress" for six weeks. She knows
exactly which chapter is next.

**A typical day.** Wakes at 9 with real intent. By 11 she has cleaned the
kitchen, answered messages, and watched two videos *about* thesis writing.
The afternoon disappears into "almost starting." At 23:00 the guilt peaks;
she plans tomorrow perfectly and the cycle repeats. Panic finally produces
work — two weeks before each committee meeting, at high cost.

**Tools tried.** Notion dashboard (three days building it, then avoided);
Forest (worked during exam crunch, but "you have to have started to use
it"); a quotes app (deleted after the fifth identical paywall prompt).

**What she needs from Wake.** The 14:00 spoken pulse to puncture the
"almost starting" trance; a widget that makes 9-to-23 feel finite by
afternoon, not at midnight; a two-minute contract small enough to beat
"open the document" dread; a re-entry that costs nothing after her
inevitable lost weeks.

**Likely voice:** The Friend (though she *thinks* she deserves The Coach —
the classic shame-driven choice our onboarding preview must handle).
**Danger states:** 23:00 guilt spiral; the app must be safe to open at that
hour (P4, P3).
**She churns if:** the app ever shows her a record of the lost weeks.

## Persona 2 — Daniel, 33 — Senior developer with a side project (S2)

**Situation.** Employed, competent, disciplined *at work* — external
structure does it for him. His side project (a SaaS he genuinely believes
in) has been "about to resume" for five months. Evenings evaporate into
YouTube and "research."

**Profile note.** Perfectionist-avoider: starting means confronting that
the codebase is six months stale; the imagined session is three hours, so
no evening qualifies. Classic construal problem — the task has no concrete
next physical action.

**Tools tried.** Todoist (his side-project list is a museum of overdue
red); TickTick with Pomodoro (fine *once started* — never solved starting);
blocking apps (he just unblocks; he's the admin).

**What he needs from Wake.** The evening widget state ("3h of today left —
enough for one real thing") to break the "not enough time to bother"
excuse; a two-minute unit that makes "just open the repo and read one file"
legitimate; a Coach voice that respects his intelligence and skips the
cheerleading.

**Likely voice:** The Coach — dry, concrete, zero pep.
**Danger states:** rationalization ("this app is too simple to work") —
countered by evidence-flavored copy and the felt result, not by claims.
**He churns if:** Wake ever feels like another system to maintain (P7), or
if the timer upsells him into sessions (P8 — he *hates* being managed).

## Persona 3 — Priya, 29 — Freelance designer, diagnosed ADHD (S3)

**Situation.** Talented, steady client work, chaotic operations: invoices
go out weeks late, portfolio unchanged for two years, taxes are a yearly
crisis. Time is genuinely, clinically hard for her to perceive: 15 minutes
and 3 hours feel identical from the inside.

**A typical day.** Hyperfocuses on client work until 16:00 without eating;
then discovers the day is "suddenly" gone. The admin tasks she avoids take
eleven minutes each when finally done — the discrepancy no longer surprises
her, but it still shames her.

**Tools tried.** Everything. Structured (beautiful; the first slipped
block "breaks" the day by 9:30); Finch (loved the kindness, drifted when
bird-care became another obligation); fifteen todo apps; phone reminders
(notification blindness within a week).

**What she needs from Wake.** Speak Time as the flagship — the only signal
that reaches her *inside* hyperfocus (auditory channel while eyes are
captured); a shape-based widget (numbers are read and skipped; shapes are
felt); one visible intention because working memory won't hold it; zero
punishment mechanics — she has a lifetime supply of shame already.

**Likely voice:** The Friend; would try The Stoic pack out of curiosity
(and must find it consent-gated and calm — P9).
**Danger states:** rejection-sensitive response to anything resembling
criticism; fast habituation (content variation is load-bearing for her).
**She churns if:** setup exceeds five decisions, or any surface implies
she failed.

## Persona 4 — Tomás, 41 — First-time founder, motivation-content heavy (S4)

**Situation.** Left a corporate job to build a company. Consumes enormous
amounts of motivational content — podcasts, "lock in" videos, Stoicism
threads — which produces the *feeling* of momentum without its substance.
His actual bottleneck: fifty cold calls and a pitch deck, both radioactive
to the touch.

**Insight he'd never volunteer.** The motivational content *is* the
procrastination — the most sophisticated avoidance he has ever built,
because it feels like preparation.

**Tools tried.** Notion company wiki (elaborate, empty), a $400 coach
(helped, unsustainable), morning-routine apps (streak died on a travel
day and the app never let him forget it).

**What he needs from Wake.** Functional content that punctures rather than
feeds the motivation loop ("You don't need to feel ready. 120 seconds.");
a Coach voice with the taste to never sound like his podcast feed; the
Start button as replacement for the "one more video then I'll start"
ritual; fresh-start framing that converts his Monday-morning ambition
spikes into actual starts (`../research/BehavioralEconomics.md` §2).

**Likely voice:** The Coach; prime candidate for The Stoic pack later.
**Danger states:** using Wake itself as motivational content to consume —
our in-app surface must be too sparse to binge (P5, P13).
**He churns if:** the content ever sounds like the generic inspiration he
can get free everywhere else.

## Cross-Persona Design Constraints (the contract these four enforce)

1. Safe at the worst hour (Maya, 23:00) — every surface passes the
   distressed-user test (P4).
2. Reaches users mid-absorption (Priya's hyperfocus; Maya's trance) —
   the auditory channel is not optional polish (Speak Time priority).
3. Respects intelligence (Daniel, Tomás) — no cheerleading, no
   feature-speak, no visible "system."
4. Survives absence (all four) — weightless re-entry is the most shared
   need across personas (P3).
5. Five-decision ceiling (Priya) — onboarding and settings simplicity is a
   hard limit, not a preference (P6).
6. Punctures rather than feeds motivation-consumption (Tomás) — content is
   functional, sparse, and points outward (P5, Content Engine rules).
